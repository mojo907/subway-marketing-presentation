Subway has about 35,800 restaurants worldwide and is still the top sandwich chain by far. But in the US, its home market, system sales fell about 6% in 2025 to $8.9 billion, after a 3.8% fall in 2024. At the same time its closest rival, Jersey Mike's, grew 13%.

So our key question is: how can Subway become the automatic lunch choice again? We answer it in five steps: the macro environment with PESTLE, then SWOT, the 5C's, segmentation, targeting and positioning, and finally the consumer decision process, before one recommendation.

PESTLE
DRAFT, to validate by Chelsea

External forces push Subway toward value, freshness and digital convenience

DefinitionPESTLE scans the macro-environment a company cannot control: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental forces.

Presented by Chelsea

PESTLE looks at the macro forces a company cannot control. For Subway, six stand out. Political: wage legislation, such as California's $20 minimum wage for fast-food workers, raises costs for franchisees. Economic: consumers are cost-conscious, which pushes all chains into a value war, while food, rent and labour costs squeeze franchisee margins. Social: growing interest in protein and fresh food fits Subway's positioning. Technological: digital ordering and delivery are now standard, and the new EMEA Blueprint format from 2027 is built around kiosks and the app. Legal: franchise rules and disclosure requirements matter because Subway has had many franchisee disputes. Environmental: sourcing and packaging are under scrutiny. Overall, the environment pushes Subway toward value, freshness and digital convenience.

Likely questions

Which factor matters most for Subway today? Economic. The value war drives its biggest move, the first-ever Fresh Value Menu (15 items under $5, April 2026).

How do wage laws affect a franchisor that owns no US stores? Subway is fully franchised, so higher wages hit franchisees, whose economics are already weak. That limits how much discounting they accept.

SWOT
DRAFT, to validate by Kira

Scale is Subway's biggest strength, but weak store economics leave it exposed

DefinitionSWOT sums up a brand's internal Strengths and Weaknesses and its external Opportunities and Threats.

Presented by Kira

SWOT sums up internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats. Subway's strengths are about scale: the largest US restaurant footprint by count, a low-cost small-format model and strong brand awareness. Its weaknesses are about economics: average sales per store are around $500,000, far below Jersey Mike's at about $1.36 million. The US base is shrinking, franchisees are strained and leadership changed again in July 2025. The opportunities are international growth, with more than 12,000 future units committed by master franchisees, plus the protein and fresh trend and growth in delivery. The threats: Jersey Mike's is growing 13% with fresh IPO capital, the whole sector is in a value war, and the sandwich category is flat, so every gain by a rival comes at Subway's expense.

Likely questions

What is the single biggest weakness? Sales per store: about $500,000 versus about $1.36 million at Jersey Mike's. It weakens franchisees and everything that depends on them.

Which opportunity is most realistic? International growth: over 30 master franchise agreements and 12,000+ committed units, including about 400 restaurants in Spain with Grupo Vierci.

Subway has global scale, but must fix franchisee profitability before Jersey Mike's takes more share

DefinitionThe 5C's analyse a brand's situation: Company, Customers, Competitors, Collaborators and Context.

Presented by Naud

The 5C's give a full picture of the situation. Company: Subway is fully franchised, owned by Roark Capital since 2024, with about 35,800 units and $16 billion in global sales. But US system sales fell about 6% to $8.9 billion in 2025. Customers: value-seeking diners, office workers and students who want price, speed, customisation and a perception of freshness and protein. Competitors: Jersey Mike's is the rising threat, with $4.3 billion in sales, up 13%, and about $1.36 million per store. McDonald's and Taco Bell attack on value. Collaborators: US franchisees are the critical partner and a strained one, while master franchisees are the new growth engine with over 12,000 future units, including about 400 in Spain with Grupo Vierci. Context: a sector-wide value war. To conclude: Subway has scale and an international pipeline, but at home it is shrinking. The key question is whether it can make franchisees profitable again before Jersey Mike's takes more share.

Likely questions

Why are franchisees strained? Average store sales are around $500,000, with an 8% royalty and a 4.5% ad fund. Franchisees of over a quarter of stores petitioned against the Sub Club offer as too generous (revised April 1, 2026), and several operators filed for bankruptcy.

Why is Jersey Mike's the main threat if Subway is six times bigger by store count? The top 15 sandwich brands' sales were roughly flat in 2025 (Guggenheim), so its 13% growth comes at Subway's expense. It also listed on the NYSE in July 2026 at a $7.3 billion valuation.

Segmentation means dividing the market into groups of consumers with similar needs. Targeting means choosing which of those groups the brand serves first. Subway's customers fall into three main segments: value-seeking diners, office workers and students who want a quick lunch, and people looking for a healthier fast-food option. Our target is urban students and young professionals aged 18 to 34. They look for three things: convenience, value and choice. Picture a student in Barcelona with 30 minutes between classes who wants something quick, filling and not too expensive. That is the lunch moment Subway is built for.

Likely questions

Which segmentation bases did you use? Demographic (age 18 to 34, students and young professionals), geographic (urban), and behavioural (the quick lunch occasion, value-seeking).

Why this target and not families? Their needs (price, speed, customisation) match what Subway offers, and they live and work where Subway stores are: stations, campuses and malls.

Consumer Decision Process Model

Subway wins the lunch decision when it is the fastest, cheapest and most flexible option

Order complete: Subway becomes the habit

Definitionthe 5 stages a consumer goes through before and after a purchase. For a fast-food lunch, it happens in minutes and is often driven by habit.

Presented by Joé

Definition. The Consumer Decision Process Model describes the five stages a consumer goes through before and after buying: (1) need recognition, (2) information search, (3) evaluation of alternatives, (4) purchase decision and (5) post-purchase behaviour. Marketers use it to understand where they can influence the customer at each step. For a low-involvement, habitual purchase like a fast-food lunch, the stages happen in minutes and the consumer often skips straight from need to purchase, which makes habit, availability and loyalty very important.

1. Need recognition. Trigger: hunger at lunchtime, a short break between classes or meetings, or a craving for something “lighter” than burgers and fries. Subway example: a student in Barcelona has 30 minutes between classes and wants something quick, filling and not too expensive. How Subway activates the need: push notifications on the app, lunchtime social media posts, and visible stores in high-traffic areas (stations, campuses, malls).

2. Information search. Internal search: memory of past visits (“I know what I order at Subway”). External search: Google Maps (“food near me”), delivery apps (Uber Eats, Glovo, Just Eat), the Subway app, TikTok / Instagram and friends’ recommendations. How Subway influences it: large store footprint means it often appears first on maps, presence on all delivery marketplaces, and menu prices and deals visible in the app.

3. Evaluation of alternatives. Key criteria for our target (time-conscious students and young professionals): price, speed, customisation, perceived healthiness / freshness, and taste. Alternatives considered: McDonald’s and Burger King (cheap and fast), Jersey Mike’s (perceived higher quality), Chipotle or salad chains (healthier), and locally a bocadillo shop or supermarket ready meal. Subway’s advantage: “freshly made, never fried”, fully customisable and with protein-rich options. Its weakness: perceived lower quality than newer sandwich rivals and strong value deals from burger chains.

4. Purchase decision. Where and how: in store at the counter (the customer builds the sandwich step by step), via the app or kiosk, or through delivery. What pushes the final choice: the Fresh Value Menu (15 items under $5, launched April 2026), Sub Club loyalty rewards, and the speed of the line at that moment. Risk factors at this stage: a long queue, a closed or empty store, or a better deal from a competitor can still change the decision at the last second.

5. Post-purchase behaviour. Satisfaction: if the sandwich was fresh, fast and good value, the customer is likely to repeat and the purchase becomes a habit. Dissatisfaction / cognitive dissonance: if the bread is dry or the price felt high, the customer may think “I should have gone to Jersey Mike’s” and leave a negative review. How Subway manages it: Sub Club points to bring customers back, app feedback, and online reviews that feed the information search of the next customers. This closes the loop back to stage 1.

Wrap. Order complete: when the experience is fresh, fast and good value, Subway becomes the habit and the customer skips the comparison next time. Tip for Q&A: hover or click a layer to bring back the detail of that stage.

Likely questions

Why do you call this a low-involvement decision? A lunch is cheap, frequent and low risk, so the five stages happen in minutes and the customer often goes straight from need to purchase. That is why habit, availability and loyalty matter so much.

At which stage can Subway gain the most? Stage 3, evaluation. Subway is already visible in stages 1 and 2. It has to convince customers that fresh and customisable is worth choosing over cheaper burger deals and higher-quality sandwich rivals.

Consumer Decision Process: conclusion

The lunch battle is won or lost at stage 3, so Subway must become the automatic choice

1Strong in stages 1 and 2: huge store network and delivery presence = always visible
2Stage 3 is the battleground: cheaper burger deals vs higher-quality sandwich rivals
3Priority: strengthen value perception (Fresh Value Menu) and loyalty (Sub Club)
4Goal: make Subway the automatic choice so customers skip the comparison
Presented by Joé

For Subway, the lunch decision is fast, habitual and highly price-sensitive. Subway is strong in the first two stages thanks to its huge store network and delivery presence, so it is almost always visible when hunger hits. The battle is won or lost in stage 3 (evaluation), where Subway must convince customers that its fresh, customisable offer is worth choosing over cheaper burger deals and higher-quality sandwich rivals. The key marketing priority is therefore to strengthen value perception (Fresh Value Menu) and post-purchase loyalty (Sub Club), so that Subway becomes the automatic, repeated choice and customers skip the comparison altogether.

Likely questions

How does Sub Club help in this model? It works on stage 5: points bring customers back, repetition builds a habit, and a habit means the customer skips stages 2 and 3 next time.

Is more discounting not a problem for franchisees? Yes, it is a real tension. Franchisees of over a quarter of stores petitioned against the Sub Club offer as too generous and it was revised on April 1, 2026. Value has to be designed so that it stays profitable for them.

Our recommendation

Subway should turn its scale into a daily habit for value-seeking urban lunch customers

1PESTLE and 5C'sDefend value: keep the Fresh Value Menu visible in a market at war on price
2SWOTProve freshness: close the quality perception gap with newer sandwich rivals
3Targeting and positioningOwn the target: urban 18 to 34s who want fast, affordable and customisable
4Consumer decision processBuild the habit: Sub Club and the app make Subway the automatic choice

Let us connect everything. PESTLE and the 5C's show a market at war on price, so Subway has to defend value and keep the Fresh Value Menu visible. SWOT shows that scale is the strength but quality perception and store economics are the weakness, so Subway has to prove freshness, not only claim it. Segmentation, targeting and positioning tell us who to win: urban students and young professionals, 18 to 34, who want fast, affordable and customisable meals. And the consumer decision process shows how: win the evaluation stage and then build the habit with Sub Club and the app. Our recommendation in one sentence: Subway should turn its scale into a daily habit for value-seeking urban lunch customers. More repeat visits mean higher sales per store, which is exactly what franchisees need.

Likely questions

How would you measure success? Sales per store (today about $500,000), repeat visits through Sub Club, and the share of orders through the app and delivery.

What is the main risk of this recommendation? Value offers that franchisees cannot afford. They already pushed back on discounting, so offers must protect their margins.
